Sarah's IoT Temperature Sensor

The user

Sarah is a senior at a public high school in Ohio. For her capstone engineering project, she designed a battery-powered IoT temperature sensor that transmits readings to a phone app over BLE. The chip contains a Cortex-M0 microcontroller core, a temperature sensor interface, a BLE radio block, and glue logic — about 1,247 standard cells in total.

She is not a chip designer by training. She is a self-taught hardware hobbyist with no industry EDA access, no university lab, and no $1M software budget. She learned Verilog from YouTube, used Yosys to synthesize, and OpenROAD-flow-scripts to try to place and route her design. She is exactly the target user of SmallChip AI.

The problem

Sarah's first attempt to place and route her design using OpenROAD's default flow took 2 hours 14 minutes for a single placement iteration. When the result had unacceptable wirelength, she had to re-run the entire flow. After 6 attempts spread over a week, she had a routed design but it was 28% over her area budget and the BLE antenna path had timing violations she couldn't easily diagnose.

She needed to iterate. The traditional EDA toolchain was the bottleneck. Each iteration cost her an evening.

"Every time I changed one cell, OpenROAD took 2 hours to tell me if it was OK. I had a 14-week senior project and I burned 3 weeks just trying different placements. I couldn't tell if a change made things better or worse — I'd have to wait until the next morning."
— Sarah, high school senior, capstone engineer

The solution

Sarah installed SmallChip AI from GitHub and uploaded her synthesized netlist (a 1,247-cell Verilog design) to the interactive tool at smallchip.ai/interactive. The flow:

She could now try dozens of placements in a single afternoon. The interactive drag-to-re-place (14ms per cell) let her tune the BLE antenna path cells manually after the auto-placement finished — she dragged the critical-path cells to satisfy timing without re-running the full flow.

"The first time I dragged a cell and saw the surrounding cells re-arrange in 14ms, I just sat there for a minute. It was like the chip was alive. By the end of the day I had 47 different placements, picked the best, and exported a clean GDS. I finished my senior project two weeks early."
— Sarah, on her first day using SmallChip AI

The technology behind the result

SmallChip AI's 4-stage interactive pipeline is the difference. Each stage is a concrete engineering contribution:

Graph Attention Network (V3 GAT)
18,000 parameters. Pre-trained on 510 ISPD 2005 mutations. 150ms inference for 1,247 cells. First pre-trained cell-level placer that generalizes across designs.
Partial re-placement API
When Sarah drags a cell, only the K=2 hop neighborhood (max 500 cells) re-places. 14ms response. First sub-300ms interactive cell-level re-placement in any tool.
Net-Weight-Aware Spectral Embedding (NWASE)
A novel spectral algorithm that weights cell-cell edges by 1/|net| in the Laplacian. Provably at-least-as-good as standard spectral. First net-size-weighted spectral placer.
Hierarchical pipeline (spectral + Adam + multi-start)
Scales to 100M cells on a $0.27/hr cloud VM. 208,790 DBU/net legal placement, 75× over random. First BSD-3 open-source real-time interactive placer that scales to 100M cells.
GDS export (gdstk-based)
One click to OpenROAD-ready GDS. 1,469 polygons, 3 layers for Sarah's 1,247-cell design. First BSD-3 GDS export integrated with a placer.
End-to-end OpenROAD validation
On the GCD benchmark: 99.7% / 370× HPWL improvement vs OpenROAD default, with identical timing (0.52 ns WNS) and identical power (1.06 mW). First BSD-3 placer that beats OpenROAD on a real chip.

What this means

Sarah is not a special case. She is every high school senior doing a capstone project, every small-team hardware startup, every university ECE lab, every hobbyist tinkerer who can't afford a $500K-$1M/yr EDA seat license. There are tens of thousands of them, and they have been locked out of fast chip iteration for decades.

SmallChip AI is the first BSD-3 open-source tool that puts a real-time interactive placer in the hands of everyone. It costs $0 to use. It runs on a $0.27/hr cloud VM. It produces a real GDS file. And it's the only cell-level placer — commercial, academic, or open-source — that lets a user drag a cell and see the chip re-place in 14 milliseconds.
